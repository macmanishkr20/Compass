Design a résumé as a document, not a dashboard. One page, portrait, a single column of content about 820px wide on a white ground, with margins wide enough to print.

Set it in a serif: the name, the summary and every bullet. Monospace is for the contact line, the dates and the years only, and the section kickers. No third face.

The order, and what each part looks like:
- A header: the name large in a serif face; under it the role in small caps, letterspaced, in the accent colour, parts separated by a middot; under that one line of contact details in a monospace face, separated by slashes, with links in the accent. A photo only if one is supplied — a small square at the left of the name. Close the header with a hairline rule.
- A summary: three or four lines of prose, the handful of things that matter set in bold — years, the money, the one claim a reader should remember.
- Each section opens with a small letterspaced uppercase kicker: EXPERIENCE, SKILLS, EDUCATION, CERTIFICATIONS.
- A role reads: the title in bold, then the employer, on one line, with the dates and the place pushed to the right margin in mono and muted. Then bullets with a hanging indent — one idea each, two lines at most, starting with what was done and ending in what came of it.
- Skills are label-and-value rows, the label in the left column: Leadership, AI, Backend, Frontend, Platform. Not a cloud of pills.

What it must not become: cards or panels around the sections, metric tiles, coloured chips for skills, sidebars, progress bars, icons standing in for words, or a second colour beyond the accent. Ink, one accent, a muted grey, and the white page.
